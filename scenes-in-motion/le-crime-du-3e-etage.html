Film · Directed by Rémi Bezançon

Le crime du 3e étage

October 6, 2026 ⚠ Spoilers
Still from Le crime du 3e étage (2026). Image © FilmAffinity. Used for commentary and analysis.

A pleasant film I'll look back on with melancholy, laughter, and suspense. It exquisitely blends an homage to Hitchcock with a nod to Shakespeare's Hamlet, adding touches of comedy and gripping suspense.

Le crime du 3e étage plays with the idea that, while Hitchcock is best known for suspense, his films also carry a romantic thread. I couldn't help recalling the ending of North by Northwest, where Roger Thornhill (Cary Grant) kisses Eve Kendall (Eva Marie Saint) and the film cuts to a train entering a tunnel, a metaphor for a romantic and sexual encounter. In those films, characters who survived life-threatening ordeals together often ended up bound romantically.

The film sets out the rules of its genre, yet teases, in a comedic register, that it might break them: a nod to Hitchcock's famous cameos, or a Bond-style moment where a spinning watch face could cut through the rope binding the leads, only to end up being useless.

Gilles Lellouche plays François, a clumsy writer who longs to be a hero but can't connect sexually, either in his writing or in his life with his partner Colette (Laetitia Casta). Guillaume Gallienne's Yann is a struggling, self-satisfied villain, all fake smiles and veiled threats. Every performance sits right in the tone of its scene.

The scene in which Colette interviews Hitchcock about suspense stirred a deep melancholy in me. On one hand, it brought back Hitchcock's unmistakable voice and his black-and-white era; on the other, I was being taught suspense by example, as Yann returns home while François is still snooping around. What I really loved about this memorable movie is that it is a love story told with an Alfred Hitchcock theme as a MacGuffin. Brilliant.

Maybe we could fall in love or solve mysteries just like in the movies. A former teacher of mine, Dr. José Ernesto Marquina Fábrega, who taught Philosophy of Physics at UNAM, used to say that life's soundtrack is off: life is badly scored. I'd add that love life is badly plotted too, since there's rarely a mystery to bring you closer to your partner.
