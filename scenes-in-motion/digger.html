Film · Directed by Alejandro González Iñárritu

Digger

⚠ Spoilers
Still from Digger (2026). Image © IMDb. Used for commentary and analysis.

I found this film overflowing with pretension and wildly over the top. Its sin is wanting to work on so many levels at once that it ends up merely mimicking a possible future in which an ecological disaster has struck the world. I don't even agree that it's a satire. González Iñárritu himself has described it as one in interviews, but the genre never materializes on screen; instead, the label becomes an excuse for the film to fail as comedy. The bad comedy that confuses the viewer in the first half is later justified through Curtis's frustration with the director, Hannah.

John Goodman is hilarious as an unhinged president, evoking his Walter Sobchak in The Big Lebowski. Jesse Plemons gives a mysterious, terrific performance, though his character spends the film constantly passing judgment on the family that profits from the disaster.

The scene where Digger meets the U.S. government fails miserably: it's little more than a series of loud, absurd outbursts, with Ganesh constantly nervous and holding back his anger. The military is portrayed as incompetent here too, but Kubrick and Peter Sellers did a far better satire of an imminent global threat to humanity in Dr. Strangelove.

From the very beginning of Digger's ten-minute monologue I was exhausted by the obnoxious loudness of every character's dialogue. I didn't find the monologue reflective or profound; it simply pointed fingers at everyone in the theater, ourselves as the audience included.

Responsibility for one's actions doesn't end with Digger Rockwell's death: his legacy has given Kenny the power to track down Curtis's relative with nothing but a phone number. González Iñárritu seems to be criticizing how tragedy can be forgiven and forgotten through whichever narrative benefits the powerful.

An overly ambitious film that becomes an awful, pointless, boring mess: relentlessly judgmental, and without a single reflection on possible solutions to our climate crisis.
