Street Fighter

October 26, 2026 ⚠ Spoilers
Still from Street Fighter (2026). Image © IMDb. Used for commentary.

Entertaining mess, soaked in nostalgia. A few things work strongly in its favor, starting with how unashamedly silly it is willing to be. David Dastmalchian’s General M. Bison is frightening and psychotic, a man who wants more power simply for the sake of having it. The costumes are fine. Chun-Li’s quest for revenge and her fighting skills are impressive, and the animated sequences, recounting both Ryu’s defeat of Sagat in Thailand and how Chun-Li lost her father because of Bison, carry a welcome Kill Bill flavor.

I liked that Ryu gets lost in his obsessions, to the point of cooking ramen so bad that neither humans nor cats will touch it. Even better is the darker thread in which his own envy drives him to destroy Ken’s invitation to a fighting tournament. 50 Cent is great as Balrog: a powerful, fearless boxer.

Blanka’s arc, on the other hand, is far too short. He is introduced as a tremendous beast capable of killing and electrocution; Ryu sets him free, and one scene later he returns as a warm, gentle soul who has suffered and only longs for his mother’s arms.

Vega’s claw is a highlight: a mysterious fighter whose mask gives him a deadly aura. He works for Bison. Many signature moves from the video game make it to the screen, and I loved that they kept E. Honda’s Hundred Hand Slap. Ken performs the Shoryuken, and Ryu unleashes a deadly Hadouken that can break through prison walls and, if not properly controlled, can unleash his inner darkness. However, I missed Dhalsim’s Yoga Fire and Yoga Flame, which were so special in the game because the opponent who took the flames was left visibly scorched for a moment.

Still, the movie is too superficial. Perhaps it only ever aimed for a narrow scope with the characters of that wonderful game. Guile is merely arrogant and obnoxious; he pulls off his signature Sonic Boom, and there is little else to him. Chun-Li’s arc is barely developed. Its biggest flaw is that it is all over the place, and it does not even have a proper ending.

It is an enjoyable ride if you played the games as a child and walk into the theater with the eyes of a ten-year-old. Beyond that, there is little left to tell; the story simply ends, without a final round.
